
# FTC 2026–2027 BIOBUZZ — Core Competition Rules

**Official source:** https://ftc-resources.firstinspires.org/ftc/game/manual

**Game:** BIOBUZZ, presented by RTX  
**Manual version:** TU06

> **Purpose:** A practical reference to the most important gameplay, scoring, robot construction, match procedure, and conduct rules. This document summarizes the manual and does not replace the official rules, Team Updates, or official Q&A.

## 1. Game Overview

BIOBUZZ is a robotics competition in which two alliances compete to score using game elements called **POLLEN** and **NECTAR**.

The field contains important scoring structures and areas, including:

- **HIVE:** A scoring structure involving HIVE CELLS.
- **FLOWER:** A scoring structure with alliance ownership and scoring conditions.
- **GARDEN:** An area where scoring elements can earn points.
- **Loading Zone:** An area used for parking points.

Teams should develop a strategy that balances autonomous scoring, driver-controlled scoring, endgame parking, and avoiding penalties.

## 2. Match Timing

A match consists of three periods:

| Period | Duration | Main rules |
|---|---:|---|
| Autonomous (AUTO) | 30 seconds | Robots operate using pre-programmed instructions. |
| Transition | 8 seconds | Robots must not make powered movements. |
| Driver-Controlled (TELEOP) | 2 minutes | Drivers control their robots. |

### Important timing rules

- Drivers cannot control the robot during AUTO, except for specifically permitted actions.
- Robots must not make powered movements during the transition period.
- Stop powered robot movement at the end of the match.
- Follow field staff instructions before entering the field or retrieving a robot.

## 3. Scoring Reference

The following is a quick reference to the scoring values identified in the game summary. Verify every value and condition against the current official manual before competition.

| Scoring action | Points |
|---|---:|
| Leave the perimeter wall during AUTO | 3 |
| Park in the Loading Zone during AUTO | 5 |
| Park in the Loading Zone during TELEOP | 5 |
| Tip a HIVE in AUTO | 20 |
| Tip a HIVE in TELEOP | 20 |
| POLLEN or NECTAR remaining in an upward-facing HIVE CELL at match end | 2 each |
| Bottom NECTAR bonus in a FLOWER | 5 |
| POLLEN or NECTAR in a FLOWER owned by your alliance | 2 each |
| POLLEN or NECTAR in your alliance's GARDEN | 1 each |
| Win a qualification match | 3 ranking points |
| Tie a qualification match | 1 ranking point |

### Scoring details to remember

- **Leaving the wall:** The robot must no longer touch the perimeter wall.
- **Parking:** At least part of the robot must be inside the Loading Zone at the applicable scoring time.
- **HIVE:** Follow the permitted method for scoring a HIVE TIP. Do not deliberately tip or damage a HIVE by another method.
- **FLOWER:** Scoring elements must meet the official scoring-volume and entry requirements.
- **FLOWER timing:** FLOWER scoring cannot begin until one minute remains in the match. Review the official rules for the consequences of early scoring.
- **FLOWER ownership:** Ownership depends on the qualifying NECTAR arrangement specified in the manual. Ownership affects which alliance receives the applicable scoring credit.
- **GARDEN:** Elements must satisfy the official zone-scoring requirements. Gardens are not protected from the opposing alliance.
- **Ranking points:** Check the current manual for the exact ranking-point thresholds and match-ranking calculations applicable to the event.

## 4. Robot Size and Construction

### Size limits

- **Starting configuration:** The robot must fit inside an 18 × 18 × 18 inch cube, subject to the official exceptions.
- **Expanded configuration:** The robot must stay within the official expanded dimensions of 18 inches wide × 24 inches long × 29 inches high, as summarized here.
- Verify all configurations, extensions, and mechanisms against the current inspection rules.

### Construction principles

- The robot must comply with all approved-part, motor, actuator, electrical, battery, control-system, and fabrication requirements.
- The robot must remain a legal assembly and must not deliberately detach components in prohibited ways.
- Mechanisms must not intentionally damage the field, game elements, or other robots.
- Teams are responsible for checking compliance even after passing inspection.
- Review the manual's detailed construction rules before selecting or modifying any component.

### Important inspection reminder

Passing inspection does not guarantee that every aspect of a robot is legal in every configuration. A robot can still receive penalties for illegal behavior during a match.

## 5. Important In-Match Restrictions

Teams should pay particular attention to these restrictions:

- Do not use driver control during AUTO except where explicitly allowed.
- Do not make powered movements during the transition period.
- Do not continue powered movement after the match ends.
- Do not deliberately eject scoring elements out of the field.
- Do not damage, force, or improperly manipulate field structures.
- Do not intentionally damage another robot.
- Do not interfere with the opponent's autonomous period in a prohibited manner.
- Follow the restrictions governing HIVE and FLOWER interactions.
- Keep team members out of the field during a match unless their participation is specifically authorized.
- Do not climb on, hang from, or improperly manipulate field structures.
- Start in the permitted location and configuration, meet preload requirements, and follow initialization procedures.

**Rule of thumb:** If a strategy depends on exploiting a loophole, disrupting the intended game, or gaining an unfair advantage, check it with the official rules and event officials before using it.

## 6. Penalties and Referee Decisions

Potential consequences include warnings, fouls, cards, robot disabling, and disqualification.

| Consequence | General meaning |
|---|---|
| Verbal warning | A warning from event staff or the Head Referee. |
| Minor foul | A scoring penalty defined by the manual. |
| Major foul | A more serious scoring penalty defined by the manual. |
| Yellow card | A serious warning that may carry consequences into later matches. |
| Red card | A severe penalty with disqualification consequences. |
| Robot disabled | Robot outputs are deactivated as directed by the rules. |
| Disqualification | Match results and ranking-point consequences are applied as specified by the manual. |

### Penalty reminders

- The severity of a penalty depends on the specific rule and circumstances.
- Repeated or deliberate violations may be treated more seriously.
- A second yellow card within the same tournament phase can result in a red card.
- Never assume that a foul has a particular point value without checking the current manual.
- Follow referee instructions immediately and raise questions through the event's official process.

## 8. Pre-Match Checklist

Use this checklist before every match.

### Robot

- [ ] The robot has passed the required inspection.
- [ ] The starting configuration meets the size limits.
- [ ] Any expanded configuration remains legal.
- [ ] All mechanisms and electrical systems comply with the construction rules.
- [ ] No loose components or mechanisms create a safety risk.
- [ ] The robot is unlikely to damage the field or another robot.

### Field setup

- [ ] The robot is in the correct alliance starting area.
- [ ] The robot meets the required wall-contact and positioning conditions.
- [ ] Preloaded scoring elements are arranged according to the current rules.
- [ ] The robot is not attached to a field element or positioned in a prohibited way.
- [ ] The drive team has followed the field setup instructions.

### Electronics and software

- [ ] The Driver Station is connected.
- [ ] The correct OpMode is selected.
- [ ] The robot has been initialized correctly.
- [ ] The autonomous program is ready if AUTO will be played.
- [ ] The robot remains stationary after initialization as required.

### Strategy

- [ ] The team knows its AUTO plan.
- [ ] Drivers understand the transition period.
- [ ] The team has a TELEOP scoring plan.
- [ ] Drivers understand the end-of-match stopping and parking requirements.
- [ ] The team knows which actions could cause fouls or penalties.

## 9. Robot Design Priorities

When designing and testing a robot, prioritize:

1. **Legality:** Ensure every component and mechanism complies with the manual.
2. **Reliability:** Prefer repeatable actions over fragile, complicated mechanisms.
3. **Scoring efficiency:** Measure scoring rate, cycle time, and success rate.
4. **Autonomous consistency:** Test from the actual starting position and account for field variation.
5. **Driver control:** Make the robot predictable and easy to operate.
6. **Defensive robustness:** Design for normal contact without damaging other robots or the field.
7. **Inspection readiness:** Keep documentation and robot configurations easy to explain and inspect.
8. **Safety:** Protect people, electronics, batteries, and field equipment.
